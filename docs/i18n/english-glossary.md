# English Glossary

The names, voice and fixed terms of the English site (`/en/...`). Written in Phase 1 of
[english-version-plan.md](english-version-plan.md) with the shell and Home, which set the voice. Phase 2 copies from
here and never invents its own term for something listed; a term that is missing is added here first, in the same
change, so the next page finds it. Persian is the binding text: the English says what the Persian says, in natural
English, never more.

## Voice

**Who reads it.** A foreigner living in Iran (decision 1): fluent in English, maybe not in Persian, here for one
person to train their dog. Plain, warm, concrete. No marketing gloss ("world-class", "passionate", "unleash"), no
promises of results, and never "every dog takes CGC / BMC".

**American English.** behavior, center, color, program, canceled. Sentence case for headings and buttons ("Where do
we start?", "Request a free evaluation"); course titles are names and keep their Title Case ("House Dog", "Companion
Dog 2"). Contractions are welcome everywhere except legal text (privacy); the privacy page's bubbles are the dog's and
keep theirs. A button or label named inside a sentence goes in curly quotes: press “Add to cart”. Placeholders give
examples with "e.g." ("e.g. 12.5").

**The visitor is "you".** The Persian's informal «تو» becomes a friendly "you"; there is no formal/informal split in
English. The visitor's dog is **"your dog"**, and **"it"** when a pronoun can't be avoided (the mascot is the only
"he", Tala the only "she"). Prefer repeating "your dog" or rephrasing over "it" when it reads cold.

**The dog speaks** (speech bubbles and the headlines that are his: Home's «چی می‌تونم یاد بگیرم؟»): first person,
short, spoken, a little playful, one or two short sentences. He describes how things work and how he feels; he never
gives orders and never promises. Keep the joke of the Persian, not its words. Bubbles are set in the chalk hand
(Marhey's Latin): keep them short, since the hand runs wide. The customer's dog, in his voice («رفیقم»), is **"my
buddy"**, never "my friend".

**Body copy and buttons are plain.** Paragraphs, answers (Help), legal text, form labels and buttons are plain
English, not the dog's voice. A button says what happens: "Request a free evaluation", "View course", "See this
course". Section headings may be the dog's ("What can I learn?") or plain ("Basic and advanced training").

### Numbers, dates and money

- **Digits:** Latin (`formatNumber`, `formatDigits` in `app/lib/locale-format.ts`). Phone: "0921 408 7867".
- **Dates:** Gregorian, day first: "3 October 2024", "October 2024" (`formatDate`, `formatTimestamp`,
  `formatMonthYear`). Years in a sentence are Gregorian ("since 2013", from `HEAD_TRAINER.sinceYear`).
- **Money:** amount first, then **Toman** (never "Tomans", "IRT" or "Rials"): "1,200,000 Toman", short forms
  "1.2 million Toman", "800 thousand Toman" (`formatToman`, `formatTomanShort` in `app/lib/course-format.ts`).
- **Ages:** "4 months old", "From 6 months old", "6 to 12 months old", "Any age" (`shared_course_age_*`,
  `shared_dog_age_*`).

### Links to pages that are still Persian

Since Phase 4 only the site rules («قوانین», `/rules`) and the Admin's panel are Persian. A link to them on the English
site carries the small **"in Persian"** note (`InPersianNote`), or says it to screen readers (`withInPersian`) where
there's no room. Sign-in, the evaluation request, buying and the account have English and carry no note.

### Legal

The English privacy text says once, at the top, that **the Persian version is binding** and the English is a
translation for convenience. Legal text is plain and exact, no contractions, no dog voice.
